# Layout Patterns

Select a pattern from the page's communication job and actual element count. Patterns are geometry intentions, not templates to force onto every page.

## Headline + support

Use for one primary claim with one supporting paragraph or visual. Give the headline the strongest scale and reserve whitespace around it.

## Hero metric

Use for one decisive number with a short label, context, and optional comparison. Keep secondary metrics visibly subordinate.

## Two-column comparison

Use for before/after, option A/B, problem/solution, or paired evidence. Match widths and internal anchors unless one side is intentionally dominant.

## Three-card evidence

Use for exactly three comparable categories. Equalize outer boxes, top anchors, and gaps; keep card internals aligned. Do not use when the items form a sequence.

## Vertical priority list

Use for ranked points or a small set of bullets. Align bullet/text starts, keep same-level indentation consistent, and use spacing rather than extra decorative containers.

## Timeline or process

Use only when order or causality matters. Make direction unmistakable and keep step labels parallel. Avoid treating unrelated categories as a timeline.

## Chart + takeaway

Give the chart the largest content area and place one concise interpretation nearby. Do not duplicate every chart value in prose.

## Image + narrative

Use when an image carries evidence or emotion. Keep text away from the focal subject, preserve crop intent, and maintain sufficient contrast.

## Decision page

Separate decision, options/trade-offs, and recommended next action. The requested decision should be identifiable at a glance.

## Pattern selection checks

- Does the pattern express the content relationship?
- Can every preserved element occupy a clear role?
- Does the page still have one dominant reading path?
- Can the pattern be implemented using current GenOffice elements and tools?
