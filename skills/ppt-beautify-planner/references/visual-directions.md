# Visual Directions

Preserve the current deck direction by default. When the user asks for a new style without naming one, choose one direction that serves the audience and content; do not blend several identities.

## Business briefing

Neutral background, disciplined grid, restrained accent, compact hierarchy, and high information clarity. Suitable for management reviews, plans, and operating updates.

## Swiss minimal

Strong grid, generous whitespace, crisp typography, minimal decoration, and one decisive accent. Suitable for concise strategy, product, and professional narrative pages.

## Editorial

Clear typographic contrast, asymmetrical but controlled composition, captions, and image-led pacing. Suitable for research, thought leadership, and story-rich material.

## Soft rounded

Friendly rounded containers, moderate whitespace, calm palette, and approachable hierarchy. Suitable for onboarding, education, services, and people-centered content.

## Dark technology

Dark field, restrained luminous accent, precise geometry, and dashboard-like hierarchy. Suitable for technical systems, AI, security, or infrastructure when the source deck supports a dark direction.

## Bold geometric

Large type, simple geometric blocks, deliberate contrast, and limited high-saturation color. Suitable for launches or campaigns; avoid for dense audit or financial pages.

## Selection constraints

- Audience and communication job outrank fashion.
- Existing brand colors and theme fonts remain default anchors.
- A direction changes the use of color, type, space, and shapes together; it is not a label pasted onto unchanged styling.
- For multi-page editing, keep one deck-wide direction while varying page layout patterns.
